Enter a trip and Calm Route compares several ways to drive it. Each route is scored on the moments that make driving stressful: merging onto highways, left turns with no traffic light, bridges and tunnels, busy multi-lane streets.

Then tell it how much extra time you can spare, and it picks the calmest route that fits.

      Privacy and feedback

      Your data stays with you. Your profile, places, avoided spots, roads you like and drive log are saved only in this browser. There are no accounts, no ads and no tracking, and there is no Calm Route server. You can back it all up or delete it under Your data in your profile.

      What other services see. To find routes, the addresses you type and the points of your trip go to the map service (Stadia Maps), which also sends the map pictures for the area you view. Points along your route go to the US National Weather Service for the weather on the way. For gas costs, the app asks fueleconomy.gov for the US average gas price, and if you pick your car, its make and model go there too. Google Maps or Apple Maps see your trip only when you open it there.

      Something feel off? Send feedback. A route that felt wrong, a stop it shouldn’t have picked, anything. Calm Route gets better from real drives.

      How routes are scored

      Calm Route asks an open-source routing engine for several kinds of routes at once (fastest plus its alternates, fewest turns, fewer highways, mostly back roads, no highways), then reads each route's road details: road type, lanes, speed limits, traffic lights, bridges and tunnels.

      Each stressful moment adds points, weighted by your comfort profile. The weights come from driving-anxiety research: highway merging and unprotected left turns are the most feared maneuvers, and studies also measure busy city streets as stressful, so back roads don't win automatically.

      Toll roads come from the same map data. Mark them “Never” and Calm Route asks for routes with no tolls at all. Fuel cost is an estimate: your car’s official EPA mpg (or numbers you type) and the US average gas price from fueleconomy.gov, checked twice a day, unless you type your own price.

      Dirt and gravel roads come from volunteer map data, which is patchy in rural areas. A road with no surface recorded is treated as paved, so “no unpaved roads” means none that the map knows about.

      Practice mode, pebbles and stone stacks are encouragement, not treatment. Drives are logged on your say-so, and everything stays in this browser.

      Times are typical driving times and don't include live traffic. Routing by Valhalla (FOSSGIS server), search by Photon, road data © OpenStreetMap contributors.

      Your car and gas price

      Without a car, costs are for a typical car (25 mpg in town, 32 on the highway). Pick yours to fill in its official mpg (US models, gas or diesel).

      Cost is an estimate from each road’s typical speed: stop-and-go streets at your city mpg, steady roads around 50 mph at your highway mpg, fast freeways a little more. How you drive can change it by 10 to 30%. Your car and price are saved only in this browser.
